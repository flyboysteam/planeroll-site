# Planeroll scroll prototype

A dependency-free static prototype of the planespotting landing-page sequence. The page contains an opening “Build your aircraft collection.” headline, the scene, phone UI, and a fixed footer, without corner labels or a progress bar. A CSS mouse-and-long-arrow scroll cue sits directly beneath the tagline. Its 3.6px wheel blip travels 12px in 784ms, matching the stationary arrow’s reveal duration. Both use ease-in-out in a 2800ms loop. The wheel fades in for 112ms before their synchronized motion and fades out for 112ms afterward. It stays hidden during arrow retraction and the remaining pause; there is no second wheel-only cycle. The cue enters 250ms after the phone entrance finishes (4250ms after the scene is ready); the mouse and wheel use 20% black while the arrow uses 35% black, and the cue fades away permanently on the first scroll until the page is refreshed. A diagonal background overlay fades from 70% black at the top-left corner to transparent at 6.0697% of the viewport width and 42.2407% of its height, using the recorded editor selection. Its angle and fade distance are recalculated on resize (170.544° and approximately 562.688px at 1523×1314). The large yellow (#FFDE0A) headline is left-aligned 96px from the viewport top and left, within 50% of the browser width. The headline uses −0.035em letter spacing. “You spot. We identify.” sits below it with a 12px layout gap in heavy sans-serif at half the headline’s font size, in solid black, without glow or chromatic effects. Both lines share an opacity that decreases directly with scrolling from scene progress 0 to 0.26, disappearing before the phone finishes rising at 0.37; reverse scrolling restores it. The footer uses an 85% black background with 12px backdrop blur, the supplied effects wordmark on the left, and Privacy Policy, Terms of Use, Flyboys, LLC, Contact, and an X icon on the right. Flyboys, LLC opens `https://flyboys.dev` in a new tab. Contact opens `mailto:tower@planeroll.app`; X opens `https://x.com/planeroll` in a new tab. Privacy Policy and Terms of Use link to the static legal pages. The footer logo links back to the landing page. The footer wordmark is 46.58px tall, with 48px vertical footer padding and 96px horizontal padding on desktop. The footer stacks below 1200px and includes safe-area padding. Apple’s official white “Download on the App Store” SVG is centered in the desktop footer at 60px tall and 15% opacity. A full-opacity yellow label, “EARLY 2027”, overlaps its upper-right corner at top −5px / right −12px with 10px monospaced black text, 3px vertical / 6px horizontal padding, a 5px radius, and a +7° rotation. Its yellow container has red/green edge offsets of −2px/+2px at 35% opacity and a subtle yellow glow with 6px/16px blurs at 20%/12% opacity. The badge is informational until an App Store destination is supplied. The footer logo uses the original static SVG on every page, with no intro or recurring animation.

## Run

From the repository root:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 4173
```

Open `http://localhost:4173/` (the previous `/?scene` URL also works). After the one-time entrance, scroll down to run the sequence and up to reverse it.

## Legal pages

`/privacy-policy/` and `/terms-of-use/` contain static HTML copied from the mobile app’s `constants/legalDocuments.ts`. The app’s `lib/legalMarkdown.ts` parser preserves its heading hierarchy, paragraphs, bullet lists, bold emphasis, hard breaks, and link destinations. The pages use a centered 640px desktop column, the same airport photograph fixed behind the page at 11% opacity over black, 75% white regular-weight body text (15/24px), full-white titles (23/26px) and headings (19/24px and 16/24px), and the app’s spacing values. Headings and bold emphasis retain their bold weight. Document links are yellow with underlines on hover or keyboard focus. The shared fixed footer stays visible; measured bottom spacing keeps the last paragraph clear of it. Narrow screens use 16px text gutters.

The source documents are working drafts. Their draft status is preserved, while bracketed editorial notes, the unfilled effective-date line, and the placeholder newsletter-provider entry are omitted from the website. The sync script applies the same filtering on subsequent imports without modifying the mobile app’s source documents. The published pages retain that working-draft status.

To refresh the copies from the sibling mobile app, run `node scripts/sync-legal.mjs` using Node.js with native TypeScript support (the current environment uses Node 26). An optional argument selects a different mobile app repository path. The generator also copies the current footer markup from `index.html` into both pages. Shared footer styles live in `assets/site-footer.css`; legal-page styles and footer measurement live in `legal/`.

## Sequence

On page load, a 75% black scrim covers the scene and phone. Once the first scene frame is ready, it holds for 500ms and fades out over 1200ms with cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1) easing. After the scrim finishes, a 250ms pause precedes the text sequence. At 1950ms, the yellow headline fades in and moves down 16px over 900ms using the same easing. The black tagline performs the identical reveal 250ms later. After both finish (3100ms), the phone rises from entirely below the viewport to its existing half-visible starting pose over 900ms. The entrance offset is composed with scroll movement, and the viewfinder uses the resulting device position so it stays aligned. This entrance runs once per load and never intercepts input. Restored scroll positions skip the text/device entrance. Reduced-motion mode removes the entrance delays and movement and completes the scrim fade immediately.

The pinned sequence has 420vh of scene travel, a fixed 1,000px centred-photo hold, and an appended livery scan with a 250px completed-render hold. The phone stays perfectly upright. Its display targets 402×874 CSS pixels, matching the 1206×2622 app capture at 3×, with a 416×888px outer shell. It is fixed-size on roomy desktops and scales down uniformly to keep at least 32px above the centred device and between it and the fixed footer, with 24px horizontal margins. The preferred minimum outer height is 440px; below 504px of available height above the footer (or in a very narrow window), fitting takes priority over that soft minimum. Its size is recalculated on resize and does not change during scrolling. The framing pose uses the same 32px vertical margins. Motion and camera projection read the shell’s actual dimensions. It starts halfway below the viewport, rises toward the chosen aircraft, frames it, and simulates a shutter press with two black shutter halves closing inside the viewfinder. At the closed-shutter snap, the camera is replaced by a black review screen with a frozen photo centred at 90% of the phone screen width (5% space per side), with rounded corners, a 1px inset white stroke at 50% opacity in Overlay blend mode, and a 2:3 aspect ratio. The shutter reopens onto that photo. Once open, the photo pulses between 50% and 100% opacity on a 2.2-second loop, with yellow, uppercase “ANALYZING...” text above it in a regular-weight monospace font scaled with the device, using the app’s red/green chromatic fringes and separate near/wide yellow phosphor layers behind crisp text. The glow uses 10/18pt shadows at 31.5%/15.75% opacity, with 2/5pt blur on the glow copies only; all values scale with the device. Six steady glow layers behind the photo use 2, 6, 12, 24, 48, and 64px blurs at 30%, 27%, 24%, 21%, 18%, and 15% opacity respectively, using a lightened blue-gray sampled from the captured tarmac. The first five layers have zero spread; the outer 64px layer has an 8px spread (one-eighth of its blur). The analyzing text pulse starts 150ms after the photo, using the same opacity range, duration, and easing; the glow stays steady. The loading treatment follows the existing crossfades, stops animating when hidden, and stays static for reduced-motion preferences. The website airfield starts dimming at the snap and settles at 11% opacity over black before the photo-to-planespot crossfade begins, keeping the airport faintly visible through the final scene. The device reaches the centre of the available area above the footer with the captured photo, holds that pose for 1,000 CSS pixels of native scrolling, then crossfades the photo into the populated planespot screen. The hold is inserted after centring and stays 1,000px at every viewport height.

Scene positioning and blends are calculated from scroll position. Reversing the scroll restores the captured photo and airfield, then crossfades the photo into the live camera over the capture interval with the shutter blades held open. This return path stays active through small direction changes until the camera is fully restored before the shutter-press interval; a fresh downward pass can then capture again. Forward capture mirrors the app onboarding’s cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1) easing and 180:120:240 close/hold/open proportions. The shutter covers only the viewfinder while closing. While fully closed at the snap, its clipping bounds switch to the captured photo with a 1px bleed on each edge, preventing exposed image strips as it reopens.

The background, camera feed, and captured photo use the supplied replacement terminal image (`krea-9e1d8306-93d7-4c8e-9b84-b147994b3a14.png`), targeting the central Delta aircraft’s fuselage at scene coordinates (1725, 1160), so the body sits at the final viewfinder’s vertical center. The camera shows a separate, wider crop than the area directly behind the device. Its zoom stays fixed at `scene.captureWidth` throughout the scroll. The feed is tied directly to the phone’s actual x/y position: one browser pixel of phone movement produces one opposite browser pixel of image movement inside the phone, keeping the viewed scene stationary in browser space. There is no separate camera easing curve, catch-up motion, or per-frame edge clamp. The aircraft starts outside the viewfinder and enters from the top as the phone rises. Exceptionally tall windows use one constant movement ratio for the whole path to stay within the available image. Reverse scrolling retraces the same positions.

The camera’s foreground continues beyond the original photo using a generated ground extension, aligned to the existing sill and pavement. A cached composite preserves the original photo throughout the final field of view and starts its foreground blend below the final frame; that boundary follows the camera aim, so capture still matches the live view after framing adjustments. This adds space to look down without mirrored ground or a movement clamp. The background and captured photo use the supplied photograph; the final planespot thumbnail is part of the supplied app screenshot. This remains a flat image projection, without depth-based parallax.

The camera chrome is a web facsimile of the actual Planeroll Add Plane screen, with the supplied hat centered horizontally and vertically in the header below the status area, at 33% of the available screen width with proportional auto height, a portrait 4:3 grid, translucent black header/footer without blur, and a centred white shutter. The feed extends behind both panels; only the sky is reflected above the image beneath the translucent header. The panels overlap the clear window by 1px to prevent fractional-pixel seams. The app’s title, close icon, tail-number hint, photo-library button, and Skip control are omitted for this prototype. Its layout and tokens are taken from the sibling `planeroll` project’s `app/(add-plane)/add-plane.tsx`, `constants/theme.ts`, and shared primitives. Browser SVG icons approximate native SF Symbols.

The planespot reveal uses the supplied 1206×2622 `blank.png` and `operator.png` app captures, copied unchanged to `prototype/planespot-a350-900-blank.png` and `prototype/planespot-a350-900-operator.png`. The captures supply the aircraft, runway, metadata, navigation, and CRT effects. Both screenshots share the phone screen’s exact aspect ratio and alignment, so each capture fits proportionally without cropping or distortion. The live clock, status icons, and Dynamic Island stay above it. All displayed content comes directly from the latest captures, including Aster International Airport, N350PR, 617+ built, 332–352 passengers, and 9,782 MI range. The photo-to-planespot crossfade first shows the before-state screenshot, followed by the existing settled-screen pause. The app’s `AircraftScanReveal` / `ScanlineBeam` treatment then reveals the livery from top to bottom: a 2pt yellow core, a 3.6pt near halo at 25%, and a 6pt outer halo at 10% with 4pt blur. Its opacity ramps up through the first 33% of travel and down through the last 33%. One CSS pixel of page scrolling moves the beam one rendered pixel, accounting for device scaling; it stops with scrolling and retraces precisely in reverse. Only the artwork above source-image row 1728 changes, leaving the metadata and navigation stationary. Reduced Motion uses a scroll-driven crossfade without the beam. The completed livery holds for a final 250px of scroll. The blank capture covers the unrevealed artwork beneath the beam; the operator capture supplies the revealed livery and the stationary metadata and navigation. Scrolling upward restores the blank along the same boundary. The preceding photo has a simulated analyzing state. The supplied `no-text.png` capture, copied unchanged to `prototype/planespot-a350-900-no-text.png`, supplies the clean metadata background from source rows 1728–2344. The texture, separators, and thumbnail remain intact. Static regions from the operator capture preserve the spot count and six specification labels. Twelve clipped regions from that same operator capture reveal the operator, airport, date, registration, manufacturer, model, and six specification values. Every region stays aligned to the full screenshot; only its clipping boundary moves, so the texture never stretches or slides. On the first positive scan progress, the text reveals from left to right over 700ms with cubic-bezier(.4, 0, .2, 1) ease-in-out. Rows start 90ms apart; fields on the same row reveal together. This timed reveal runs once per page load and stays revealed through reverse scrolling; refreshing resets it. Reduced Motion uncovers all details immediately when the scan begins. The earlier tinted and blurred concealment rectangles are removed.

The shared status bar follows [Apple’s iOS 27 visual reference](https://www.apple.com/os/ios/), using system semibold text and vector cellular, Wi-Fi, and rounded battery indicators. The clock shows the viewer’s local time, follows the browser locale’s 12/24-hour convention without AM/PM, and updates at each minute boundary independently of scrolling. It resynchronizes when the page regains focus, becomes visible, or returns from the back/forward cache. Signal and battery remain illustrative full-strength/full-charge states. This is a browser facsimile; the native system font is used on Apple devices.
